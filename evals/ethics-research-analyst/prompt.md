Our compliance team wants a second opinion. Maya Ortiz, CFA, is a senior equity analyst at Brightline Securities. Here's what happened this quarter:

1. At a private dinner, TerraNova Mining's CFO told Maya that the company will announce next week that its largest mine is closing. The next morning Maya sold the TerraNova shares in her father's account, which she manages for free.
2. Maya combined public satellite images of competitor CopperPeak's sites, her own store visits and minor, non-material comments from CopperPeak's suppliers, and concluded CopperPeak will beat earnings. She issued a buy recommendation.
3. Before Brightline distributed that buy recommendation to clients, Maya bought CopperPeak shares for her own account.
4. A wealthy client offered Maya a two-week vacation package if the client's portfolio beats its benchmark next year. Maya accepted and did not tell Brightline.
5. Maya's colleague John Park, who passed Level II of the CFA exam last year, signs his emails 'John Park, CFA Level II'.
6. Maya refers clients to a tax attorney who pays her a fee for each referral. She told Brightline but not the clients.

For each item, tell us whether it violates the CFA Institute Code and Standards, which standard, and what should be done.
